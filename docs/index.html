CRITICAL STATUTORY WARNING

Read This First.

“A person may file a petition for expungement of a conviction record under this chapter only once in their lifetime.”

Unlike states with rolling expungement statutes, Indiana strictly limits conviction record expungement to a single proceeding in your lifetime.

The 365-Day Consolidation Window

Under IC § 35-38-9-9(d), if you have convictions across different Indiana counties (e.g. Marion & Huntington), all petitions must be filed within 365 days of the first filing.

Permanent Forfeiture of Omitted Cases

If you neglect to include any conviction in your petition, you permanently forfeit the right to ever expunge that conviction for the remainder of your life.

Under the Hood

Technical Architecture

Every component runs client-side in your browser. No server ever touches your data.

🔍

Knockout.js Observable Scraping

The content script reads Indiana MyCase's Knockout.js view-model directly from ko.dataFor() on the rendered DOM — bypassing brittle CSS selector scraping entirely. The Odyssey portal binds every case, charge, and disposition event into a reactive observable tree. By reading that observable model in-memory, the tool is far more resilient to minor template re-renders than any selector-based approach.

When the KO model is unavailable (e.g., a non-Knockout page element), the scanner falls back to a structured DOM traversal using stable ARIA roles and data attributes, then falls back again to a CCS fetch() call with credentials: 'same-origin' and a 800–1500 ms jitter delay to retrieve case detail JSON from the Odyssey public API.

ko.dataFor(document.querySelector('[data-bind]'))
⚖️

IC § 35-38-9 Statutory Decision Engine

The eligibility.js rules engine is a self-contained IIFE (no ES module imports) that evaluates each case against all five statutory tiers — IC §§ 35-38-9-1 through 35-38-9-5. It computes exact integer day counts from conviction/sentencing dates, applies per-tier mandatory vs. discretionary standards, enforces statutory disqualifications (violent offenses, sex offenders, public servants), and runs the cross-county 365-day consolidation safety check under IC § 35-38-9-9(d).

Section 1 non-conviction records (arrests, dismissals, acquittals) are explicitly decoupled from the 365-day multi-county consolidation clock — as the clock governs only conviction tiers. The engine is tested with 20 automated Jest assertions covering edge cases including leap years and boundary conditions.

assessEligibility(caseType, convDate, charges, priorExpungements)
📄

In-Browser PDF Generation (pdf-lib)

All 10 court pleadings are assembled client-side using the vendored pdf-lib.min.js library — no server, no upload, no cloud rendering. The PDF context engine (pdf-generator.js) enforces Indiana Trial Rule 10 formatting constraints: 8.5×11 in (612×792 pt), 1-inch margins, 12 pt minimum font size, Times New Roman/Georgia families, double-spaced body text, single-spaced tables, and page numbers bottom-center.

Interactive fillable form fields are embedded using pdf-lib's PDFAcroForm API so petitioners can type directly into the PDF before printing — eliminating hand-correction errors on formal pleadings.

const pdfDoc = await PDFDocument.create(); // vendored pdf-lib
🏛️

Bifurcated DOC 07 Relief (Mixed Batches)

When a petition contains both IC § 35-38-9-1 non-convictions and conviction-tier records in the same county, the Proposed Order (DOC 07) cleanly bifurcates relief into two separate statutory subsections. Section 1 records receive mandatory redaction and sealing with no civil rights restoration references (civil rights were never lost). Conviction records receive IC §§ 35-38-9-6/7 statutory access restrictions and explicit civil rights restoration under IC § 35-38-9-10. The lifetime one-shot finding under IC § 35-38-9-9(i) is only included when conviction tiers are present.

Courts reject blanket orders that apply conviction-level restrictions to dismissed charges, or that purport to restore civil rights for non-conviction records. Bifurcation eliminates this as a rejection vector.

📋

Prior Cause Number & Multi-County Consolidation

When a petitioner has already filed in one county and is now filing in a second county within the 365-day window (IC § 35-38-9-9(d)), the UI captures the prior filing date, county, and optionally the prior XP cause number (e.g., 49D01-2501-XP-000123). This data is injected as affirmative statutory averments into DOC 04 (Verified Petition) and DOC 05 (Prosecutor Notice) — transforming an unverified claim into an instantly auditable Odyssey docket reference. Prosecutors can verify the prior filing within seconds, avoiding defensive motions for clarification.

The 365-day clock uses exact integer day arithmetic — not month arithmetic — to prevent leap-year edge cases from inadvertently expiring a valid consolidation window. Indiana Trial Rule 6(A) rollover warnings trigger automatically when fewer than 14 days remain.

Math.floor((today - priorDate) / (1000 * 60 * 60 * 24)) ≤ 365
📮

Form 06 Service Method Selector

DOC 06 (Certificate of Service) pre-marks the correct service method checkbox ([X]) based on the petitioner's selection: Indiana Odyssey E-Filing System (IEFS), Certified Mail (Return Receipt Requested), or First Class U.S. Mail / Hand Delivery. Pro se filers frequently file through IEFS while leaving paper mail checkboxes ticked, creating an ambiguous certificate of service record. The synchronized selector closes this gap between Indiana Trial Rule 5 requirements and actual filing method.

🔒

Zero-Server Privacy Architecture

There is no backend. No API. No cloud function. All scraping, eligibility analysis, PDF compilation, and form generation executes entirely inside the user's browser tab using native Web APIs and the vendored pdf-lib library. Petitioner PII (SSN, DOB, DL#, 10-year address history) is persisted in chrome.storage.local (extension) or localStorage (web app) — never transmitted anywhere. The content script's CCS fetcher uses credentials: 'same-origin' exclusively, ensuring no credentials leak outside the user's active MyCase session.

🧪

Automated Test Suite & Dual-Tree Parity

The codebase maintains two parallel source trees — extension/sidepanel/ (Chrome Manifest V3) and docs/app/ (standalone web app) — that must remain identical for all shared modules. A dedicated scripts/check-parity.js script enforces byte-exact equality between both pdf-generator.js trees as part of npm test. The Jest suite covers 20 statutory eligibility assertions including Section 1 decoupling, cross-county blocking, leap year boundaries, and disqualification edge cases. All tests run in under 600ms.

npm test # jest + check-parity + check-i18n + check-disclaimers
Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Costs, filing logistics, privacy, and the most common pitfalls under Indiana's Second Chance law.

How much does an expungement cost in Indiana if I represent myself?

Filing Fees: Under Indiana law, petitions for non-convictions (arrests, dismissals, dropped charges under IC § 35-38-9-1) require $0 in court filing fees.

For convictions (misdemeanors or felonies under §§ 35-38-9-2 through 5), standard Indiana trial courts charge a single civil filing fee (typically ~$157), regardless of how many cases you consolidate in that county. If you receive public assistance or have low income, you can submit the included DOC 08 Fee Waiver Petition to ask the judge to waive this fee entirely under IC § 33-37-3-2.

Where do I send and file my completed expungement documents?

Under Indiana Code § 35-38-9-8 and Indiana Trial Rules, your expungement paperwork must be directed to specific statutory recipients:

  • The Trial Court (County Clerk): File your original Appearance Form (DOC 01), Notice of Exclusion & Confidential Sheet (DOC 02 & 03), Verified Petition (DOC 04), Proposed Order (DOC 07), and optional Fee Waiver (DOC 08) with the Circuit or Superior Court Clerk in the county where your conviction occurred. This can be done in person at the clerk's window or through the Indiana E-Filing System (IEFS).
  • The Prosecuting Attorney: Under IC § 35-38-9-8(e), serve a copy of the petition and Notice of Filing (DOC 05) on the County Prosecuting Attorney via Certified U.S. Mail or IEFS e-service. The prosecutor has 30 days to review the petition and file an objection or consent.
  • State Repositories (ISP & BMV): When the judge grants and signs your Proposed Order (DOC 07), certified copies must be served on the Indiana State Police Criminal History Repository and the Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles so their central databases are officially sealed and driver records are updated.
  • Local Law Enforcement: The Order must also be served on the County Sheriff and arresting police agencies to seal local arrest logs and detention records.

Tip: The Indiana Expungement Assistant extension includes a built-in Filing & Service Address Directory on the Generate tab with verified physical addresses, phone numbers, and 1-click clipboard copy for Indiana counties and statewide repositories.

What is the difference between "Mandatory" and "Discretionary" expungements?

Under Indiana Code § 35-38-9-2 (misdemeanors) and § 35-38-9-3 (Level 6/Class D felonies without injury), the statute specifies that the court SHALL grant the expungement if you meet the statutory prerequisites (waiting period passed, fines paid, no pending charges). The judge has no legal discretion to deny a compliant petition.

For higher-level felonies (IC § 35-38-9-4), the statute says the court MAY grant the petition. The trial judge weighs evidence of rehabilitation, character letters, and prosecutor objections before making a discretionary ruling.

Can traffic infractions, speeding tickets, or driver's license points be expunged?

No. Indiana Code § 35-38-9 only applies to criminal offenses (arrests, misdemeanors, and felonies). Civil traffic infractions (IF cause numbers on MyCase, like standard speeding tickets) are not criminal offenses and cannot be expunged under this law.

However, criminal traffic offenses—such as Operating While Intoxicated (OWI/DUI), Driving While Suspended (Class A Misdemeanor), or Reckless Driving—are criminal convictions and can be fully expunged once statutory waiting periods are met.

Is my personal data or court history ever uploaded to a server or cloud database?

Never. The entire assistant runs 100% locally in your browser. The tool parses public records directly from your active MyCase session, and all 10 PDF court forms are compiled on your machine using pdf-lib.

There are zero external tracking servers, zero databases, and zero analytics scripts capturing your data. Your criminal history, SSN, and personal identifiers remain strictly on your computer.

Do I need an official background check before filing?

Yes, it is strongly recommended. While MyCase contains most public court records, relying solely on it can be risky. Under IC § 35-38-9-9(i), you only have one lifetime opportunity to file for conviction expungement.

If a case was archived, expunged previously but incorrectly recorded, or originated in a county before they transitioned to MyCase, it might not appear in a standard search. We strongly recommend obtaining an official Indiana State Police (ISP) Criminal History Background Check to ensure absolutely every charge is accounted for before generating your final packet.

What happens if I accidentally forget to include a case in my petition?

This is the single most dangerous mistake under Indiana's expungement statute. Under IC § 35-38-9-9(i), you may only file for conviction expungement once in your lifetime.

If you omit a conviction, you permanently forfeit the right to ever expunge that record in the future. That is why our assistant includes a mandatory 4-point pre-filing audit checklist requiring you to search under prior names and across all 92 Indiana counties before generating your court petition.

Can an employer or landlord ask about or discriminate against an expunged record?

Under IC § 35-38-9-10, it is unlawful discrimination for an employer, licensing board, or housing provider to discriminate against an individual based on an expunged record. Furthermore, job applications in Indiana cannot ask about expunged records, and you may legally state that the arrest or conviction never occurred.
